**Sanjoaninas** is the Azores' biggest secular festival, full stop. For ten days every June, Angra do Heroísmo's UNESCO-listed historic centre is taken over by marches, concerts, bullfights, street food, fireworks, and a level of all-night activity the rest of the year doesn't see. **In 2026 it runs from Friday, 19 June through Sunday, 28 June.**

The 2026 theme is **"Angra e a Açorianidade"** — chosen to coincide with the **50th anniversary of Azorean Autonomy**. The phrase comes from local writer Tomé Ribeiro Gomes; the festival poster, by Rúben Quadros Ramos, puts a female figure at the centre personifying Autonomy in a moment where past, present, and future meet.

Below: what to expect, where to be on which night, and how to navigate ten days of programming.

### The four stages, in one paragraph

There are **four music stages** running through the festival. Three are **free**; one is **ticketed**:

- **Palco MEO — Cerrado do Bailão** *(ticketed, €30 weekly wristband)*. This is where the headline acts play. Nine nights of concerts and DJs, the most consistent late-night programming, and the only stage you need a wristband for.
- **Palco Continente — Praça Velha** *(free)*. The town's main square; gravity well of the festival.
- **Palco Expert — Largo Prior do Crato** *(free)*. Quieter, square in front of the old palace; tends toward fado, philharmonics, and local acts.
- **Palco Guarita — Cais da Alfândega** *(free)*. Down at the marina/waterfront end. Cooler air, sea views.

The **€30 weekly wristband** is the only paid entry and it covers the entire ticketed lineup at Bailão. The price hasn't moved in over a decade — partly a deliberate municipal choice, partly the reason the festival continues to draw such a strong crowd from the diaspora and other islands.

### Who's headlining

The 2026 Bailão lineup, in alphabetical order:

- **Vizinhos** — the year's headline pop draw.
- **Matias Damásio** — Angolan-Portuguese romantic pop, packs out the Bailão every time he comes through the Azores.
- **Mariza** — fado at its biggest stage. A statement booking for the festival's 50-year-of-Autonomy theme.
- **Wet Bed Gang** — Lisbon rap collective.
- **Ivo Lucas** — pop singer/songwriter.
- **Sippinpurpp** — Portuguese hip-hop.
- **Irina Barros** — popular romantic ballad singer.
- **Nuno Ribeiro** — singer-songwriter.
- **Uzhoms** and **Art'Fado** — local Terceira bands, both with strong followings on the island.

Total musical investment is reported at roughly **€430,000** — and the organisers' deliberate skew toward "consagrados" (established Portuguese acts) over big international names is what makes the €30 weekly price possible.

### The traditional core: marches, masses, and the procession

Strip away the modern stages and Sanjoaninas is still, fundamentally, the **festas em honra de São João Baptista** — the patron saint of Terceira. The religious and traditional programme runs alongside the music:

- **Marchas Populares (the São João marches).** This is the night the locals point you to. Each freguesia or association puts together a costumed troupe with original choreography, music, and props; they march in sequence through the historic centre — **Alto das Covas → Rua da Sé → Rua de São João → Rua de Minhas Terras → Rua Direita → Praça Velha**. In 2026 there are around **37 marches**, including **five children's marches**, so the parade has been split across **two nights — June 23 (Tuesday) and June 24 (Wednesday)**. June 23, *Noite de São João*, is by tradition the longest and rowdiest night of the year in Angra.
- **Missa de São João** at the **Sé** (the cathedral), on the saint's feast day (June 24).
- **Missa de Coroação** — Coronation Mass — at the Sé, bringing together **38 impérios** from across the island in one place. A rare moment when so many of the island's brotherhoods gather in one church.
- **Procissão de São João Baptista** — major religious procession through the city centre with **flower carpets** (*tapetes de flores*) laid down on the streets and a long human chain accompanying the saint.

If you've only been on the island for the music, the procession alone is worth planning around — the *tapetes de flores* are laid down by hand the night before and the chain of people that walks the route is the whole town.

### The bullfighting side

Sanjoaninas is also the headline of Terceira's bullfighting season. There are two distinct strands:

**Feira Taurina de São João** at the **Praça de Toiros da Ilha Terceira** — four nights, 21–24 June. The bigger fights of the season:

- **Sunday, 21 June** — Corrida à Portuguesa. Cavaleiros: **Tiago Pamplona, João Moura Jr., João Ribeiro Telles**.
- **Monday, 22 June** — Grande Corrida Mista. Cavaleiros **João Pamplona, João Moura Jr.**; matador **Marco Pérez**.
- **Wednesday, 24 June** — Corrida Mista de São João. Cavaleiros **João Moura Jr., João Ribeiro Telles**; matador **Fernando Adrián**.

**Touradas à corda** — the rope-bullfighting that's specifically Terceiran — are scheduled across the festival days too, in different parts of the city and outlying parishes. ### Food: tasquinhas everywhere

Throughout the festival, the squares fill with **tasquinhas** — small stalls run by local associations, parish committees, and clubs. This is the cheapest and best way to eat in Angra during the festival. What to look for:

- **Alcatra** — Terceira's signature beef stew, slow-braised in a clay pot with red wine, chouriço, bacon and onions. Often the headline plate at the bigger tasquinhas.
- **Sopas do Espírito Santo** — beef-and-mint broth over bread, still being served in the carry-over from the May Espírito Santo weekends.
- **Bifes da grelha** and **prego no pão** — quick standards.
- **Queijadas da Dona Amélia** — small cinnamon-and-molasses tarts, the island's signature pastry, named after Queen Amélia who visited in 1901.
- **Vinho de cheiro** by the jug; the local volcanic wines of Biscoitos are also widely poured.

### Fire, fado, fireworks, sport

The full programme stretches well beyond the music and the bullfights:

- **Icarus et Lumen** — a moving fire-and-light street performance that snakes through the city in the early evening.
- **Grande Noite de Fado** at Palco Expert (Largo Prior do Crato) — a single big fado night with **Guida Costa**, **Lisete Silva**, and **Fábio Ourique**. Usually around 22:30.
- **Feira de São João** — the broader street fair, separate from the bullfighting fair, with crafts, stands, and rides for kids.
- **Sports** programming — the Câmara runs a competitive sports strand alongside the cultural one (typically football, swimming, traditional games).
- **Fireworks** punctuate the bigger nights.

### How to plan a night (a few suggestions)

If you've got one night and you want the **biggest possible Sanjoaninas experience**, that's **June 23, Noite de São João**. Marches through the historic centre, all four stages active, fireworks, tasquinhas everywhere, the longest night of the year in Angra by some distance.

If you want the **headline pop concert**, watch the Bailão schedule for Vizinhos, Matias Damásio, or Mariza — and buy the **€30 weekly wristband** at Ticketline (or at the gate). It pays for itself in a single night.

If you want the **religious tradition** without the all-night party, **June 24** (St. John's feast day) — daytime mass at the Sé, the coronation, the procession with flower carpets, the children's marches.

If you want the **bullfights at the Praça de Toiros**, book tickets early — the four-night Feira Taurina sells through.

If you want the **rope bullfighting** (tourada à corda), the festival days have several scheduled across the city — check the [tourada calendar]({{ '/special/' | relative_url }}) we publish from the Papelaria 96 list.

### Practical notes

- **Where to be**: most of the action is concentrated in the **historic centre** between Praça Velha, Rua da Sé, Rua de São João, Rua Direita, and down to Cais da Alfândega. It's all walking distance.
- **Where to stay**: book months ahead. Sanjoaninas brings back diaspora from California and Massachusetts, and pulls visitors from the other islands; the city books up.
- **Driving**: the centre is closed off and parking gets impossible by mid-afternoon on the busier nights (23, 24, 27). Park outside and walk in, or use the municipal lots.
- **Bring cash** for the tasquinhas and bullring; cards work at Bailão and most newer venues but the smaller stalls and the bullring boxes are still cash-first.
- **Kids are welcome everywhere except the late-night Bailão concerts**. The children's marches and the broader feira are deliberately family-oriented.
