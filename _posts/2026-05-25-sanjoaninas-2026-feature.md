**Sanjoaninas** is the Azores' biggest secular festival, full stop. For ten days every June, Angra do Heroísmo's UNESCO-listed historic centre is taken over by marches, concerts, bullfights, street food, fireworks, philharmonics and a level of all-night activity the rest of the year doesn't see. **In 2026 it runs from Friday, 19 June through Sunday, 28 June.**

The 2026 theme is **"Angra e a Açorianidade"**, chosen to coincide with the **50th anniversary of Azorean Autonomy**. The phrase comes from local writer **Tomé Ribeiro Gomes**; the festival poster, by **Rúben Quadros Ramos**, puts a female figure at the centre personifying Autonomy in a moment where past, present, and future meet.

What follows is an opinionated guide: **the full lineup we've been able to confirm**, broken down by stage, then a day-by-day walkthrough of what's already pinned for the official program. The CMAH program firms up gradually — for last-minute changes, check [sanjoaninas.cmah.pt](https://sanjoaninas.cmah.pt/) on the day.

## The four stages

There are **four music stages** running through the festival. Three are **free**; one is **ticketed**:

### Palco MEO — Cerrado do Bailão *(ticketed)*

This is where the headline acts play. **Nine nights of concerts and DJs**, the most consistent late-night programming, and the only stage you need a wristband for.

- **Weekly wristband (pulseira semanal): €30.** Covers all nine nights. The price hasn't moved in over a decade — a deliberate municipal choice that keeps the festival accessible.
- **Daily wristband: €15.**
- Tickets at [Ticketline](https://www.ticketline.pt/en/evento/sanjoaninas-2026-pulseira-semanal-104795) and at the gate.

The Bailão lineup is built around **established Portuguese names** rather than big international acts — that's how the price stays where it is. Total musical investment across the festival is reported at around **€430,000**.

### Palco Continente — Praça Velha *(free)*

The town's main square, the gravity well of the festival. Mixes local and national artists across genres. Notable Continente moments: the **Grande Noite de Fado**, the **Grande Noite de Folclore**, *cantoria* (sung Portuguese folk-improv) sessions, and a children's show.

### Palco Expert — Largo Prior do Crato *(free)*

Quieter square in front of the old palace. Programmed toward **philharmonic bands** and **traditional Portuguese music interpreters** — a more classical, contemplative slot.

### Palco Guarita — Cais da Alfândega *(free)*

Down at the marina/waterfront. The Câmara has framed Guarita this year as the **stage that gives priority to local bands** with original material — "engaging sound proposals", in their phrasing. Cooler air, sea views, generally the most adventurous booking.

## The traditional core: marches, masses, processions

Strip away the modern stages and Sanjoaninas is, at root, the **festas em honra de São João Baptista** — the patron saint of Terceira. The religious and traditional programme runs alongside the music.

### Marchas Populares (the São João marches) — a record year

This is the night the locals point you to. Each freguesia or association puts together a costumed troupe with original choreography, music, and props; they march in sequence through the historic centre — **Alto das Covas → Rua da Sé → Rua de São João → Rua de Minhas Terras → Rua Direita → Praça Velha**.

**2026 set a record**: **43 adult marches + 8 children's marches**, total **51**. The volume is why the adult parade is split across **Monday/Tuesday and Tuesday/Wednesday nights (22, 23 and 24 June)** rather than the single São João night it used to fit on.

### Philharmonic bands

The opening night's **band parade** is the most concentrated philharmonic moment — every freguesia's band rolling into the city — but they also play across the week at Continente and Expert. **Visiting bands** include the **Banda do Senhor Santo Cristo de Toronto** (Toronto diaspora) and the **Fanfarra Operária Gago Coutinho e Sacadura Cabral** (mainland Portugal).

### Missa de São João and Missa de Coroação

The cathedral mass on the saint's feast day is the religious centre. The **Missa de Coroação** is the rarer moment — **38 impérios** (the parish brotherhoods that drive the Espírito Santo festivals) converge on the Sé in a single coronation, which only happens during Sanjoaninas.

### Procissão de São João Baptista

Major religious procession through the city centre with **tapetes de flores** (flower carpets) laid down by hand on the streets the night before — a long human chain accompanies the saint along the route.

## The bullfighting side

Sanjoaninas is also the headline of Terceira's bullfighting season. Two strands:

### Feira Taurina de São João — at the Praça de Toiros (ticketed)

Four nights of the biggest fights of the year. Bilhetes through the praça's box office and the usual outlets — these typically sell through.

### Touradas à corda — the rope-bullfighting in the streets

Tourada à corda is the specifically Terceiran form — a single bull on a long rope, run through the streets by *pastores*, with spectators on every wall and porch. Several are scheduled across the festival in different parts of the city and outlying parishes. Confirmed so far: **São Bento, Tuesday 23 June, 18h00** (ganadarias RB / MJ / AL / OT). ## Food: tasquinhas everywhere

Throughout the festival, the squares fill with **tasquinhas** — small stalls run by local associations, parish committees, and clubs. This is the cheapest and best way to eat in Angra during the festival.

- **Alcatra** — Terceira's signature beef stew, slow-braised in a clay pot with red wine, chouriço, bacon and onions. The headline plate at the bigger tasquinhas.
- **Sopas do Espírito Santo** — beef-and-mint broth over bread; still being served in the carry-over from the May Espírito Santo weekends.
- **Bifes da grelha** and **prego no pão** — quick standards.
- **Queijadas da Dona Amélia** — small cinnamon-and-molasses tarts, the island's signature pastry, named after Queen Amélia who visited Terceira in 1901.
- **Vinho de cheiro** by the jug; the local volcanic wines of **Biscoitos** are also widely poured.

## Fire, fado, fireworks, sport

Beyond the music and the bullfights:

- **Icarus et Lumen** — a moving fire-and-light street performance that snakes through the city in the early evening.
- **Grande Noite de Fado** at Palco Expert with **Guida Costa**, **Lisete Silva**, **Fábio Ourique**.
- **Grande Noite de Folclore** at Palco Continente — folkloric groups from across the island, costumed dancing, accordion and viola.
- **Feira de São João** — the broader street fair, separate from the bullfighting fair, with crafts, stands, and rides for kids.
- **Sports programme** — the Câmara runs a competitive sports strand alongside the cultural one (football, swimming, traditional games).
- **Fireworks** punctuate the bigger nights (especially 23, 24, 28).

## How to plan a night

If you have **one night** and want the **biggest Sanjoaninas experience**, that's **June 23, Noite de São João**: marches through the historic centre, all four stages active, fireworks, tasquinhas everywhere, the longest night of the year in Angra by some distance.

If you want the **headline pop concert**, watch the Bailão schedule for **Vizinhos**, **Matias Damásio**, or **Mariza** — and buy the **€30 weekly wristband** at Ticketline (or at the gate). One night at €15 daily; the full week pays for itself in two.

If you want the **religious tradition** without the all-night party — **June 24** (St. John's feast day): daytime mass at the Sé, the coronation, the procession with flower carpets, the children's marches.

If you want the **bullfights at the Praça de Toiros** — book tickets early; the four-night Feira Taurina sells through.

If you want the **rope bullfighting** (tourada à corda) — São Bento on the 23rd is the festival's most central one; see [our special events]({{ '/special/' | relative_url }}) for the full June list.

If you want the **fado** — Friday 26 is the Fábio Ourique anniversary night at Palco Expert; the big multi-voice Grande Noite de Fado is on one of the weekend nights.

If you want the **folklore and philharmonics** — Continente and Expert run them most nights from 19h–21h.

## Practical notes

- **Where to be**: most of the action is concentrated in the **historic centre** between Praça Velha, Rua da Sé, Rua de São João, Rua Direita, and down to Cais da Alfândega. It's all walking distance.
- **Where to stay**: book months ahead. Sanjoaninas brings back diaspora from California, Massachusetts, and Toronto, and pulls visitors from the other islands; the city books up.
- **Driving**: the centre is closed off and parking gets impossible by mid-afternoon on the busier nights (23, 24, 27). Park outside the centre and walk in, or use the municipal lots.
- **Bring cash** for the tasquinhas and bullring; cards work at Bailão and most newer venues but the smaller stalls and the bullring boxes are still cash-first.
- **Kids are welcome everywhere except the late-night Bailão concerts**. The children's marches, the children's show at Continente, and the broader feira are deliberately family-oriented.
- **Times**: posters show Lisbon time, which is also the Azores time during summer — events happen exactly when the poster says.
